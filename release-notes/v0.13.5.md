# Mindflow v0.13.5

**Released:** 2026-09-09

## What's new

- **Dictation now picks a microphone that can actually hear you.** It used to follow whatever your system input was set to, even when that was a loopback or virtual device carrying no sound at all — the kind of thing meeting apps and audio tools leave behind. Mindflow now prefers whichever microphone last produced a transcript for you, then the best real one it can find, and never opens a loopback device on its own.
- **Settings → Dictation → Microphone** lets you choose the input yourself. Your system-wide sound settings are never changed; the choice applies to Mindflow's dictation only.
- **Pressing Escape now copies to your clipboard as well as History.** Still no paste, as before — so an accidental Escape costs you nothing, and the text is one ⌘V away.

## Fixes

- If dictation hears nothing, it now says so and names the microphone it listened to. Previously it closed without a word, which was indistinguishable from dictation being broken — and the microphone it was listening to was often not the one you expected.
- When dictation gives up, the explanation now describes only what actually happened. Several of its messages could previously state things that were not true of your session, including claiming retries that had not succeeded and promising behaviour that could not occur.
- VoiceOver no longer announces "Mindflow is reconnecting" during the periods when nothing is being retried.
- The Settings screen advertised the wrong default dictation shortcut. It is ⌃⌥Space (Control + Option + Space), not the four-key combination previously shown.
- Bluetooth headsets are now used last rather than first when Mindflow chooses for you. Opening a headset's microphone switches it to phone-call audio quality for as long as dictation runs, which is worth avoiding when a wired or built-in microphone is available. You can still pick your headset explicitly.

## Under the hood

- Quitting immediately after pressing Escape now waits for that dictation to finish saving rather than terminating on top of it.
- A remembered microphone that stops producing transcripts is forgotten after two consecutive silent dictations, so an unplugged device cannot pin the choice permanently.
- The wording of dictation's error messages is now derived from a record of what happened, in a module that cannot see settings or policy — so a message has no way to describe behaviour it cannot observe.
